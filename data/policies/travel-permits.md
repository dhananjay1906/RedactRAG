# Travel Permits Policy

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy SUP-230

## Purpose

This policy describes when a person under supervision must obtain permission to travel, how to apply, what it costs and how long each type of permit lasts. Travel without a required permit is a violation of supervision conditions.

## Travel That Needs No Permit

A client may travel freely within Harbor County and the three adjacent counties (Marlow, Pinecrest and Tidewater). A client may also take day trips of up to 100 miles from the home address without a permit, provided they return the same day and check in with their officer by text before leaving if they are at the high-risk supervision level. Travel for regularly scheduled work, medical care or court appearances in the adjacent counties never needs a permit.

## Out-of-County Overnight Travel

Any overnight stay outside the four-county area requires a permit. The client files Form T-7 with their officer at least 10 working days before the planned departure. The form must list the destination address, the dates, the name and phone number of the host or hotel, and the reason for the trip.

An officer may approve overnight permits of up to 14 consecutive nights. Permits longer than 14 nights and up to a maximum of 30 nights must be approved by the unit supervisor. No travel permit may be issued for more than 30 nights in a single request, and a client may not hold permits totaling more than 60 nights in any 12-month period.

The permit fee for out-of-county overnight travel is $10. The fee for out-of-state travel is $25. Fees are waived for clients who qualify under the financial hardship policy, and for any travel needed for medical treatment.

## Out-of-State Travel

Out-of-state travel requires Form T-7 plus Form T-9, the destination state notification. The Department must give the receiving state's supervision office at least 7 working days of notice. For this reason, out-of-state requests are accepted only if filed at least 15 working days before departure. Clients who have not completed their first 6 months of supervision are not eligible for out-of-state permits, except for medical emergencies and funerals.

Clients under supervision for a sex offense or a violent felony may not travel out of state without the written approval of the Chief Probation Officer, regardless of how long they have been supervised.

## International Travel

International travel is not permitted while a person is under supervision unless a judge signs an order that specifically approves the trip. The client must apply to the court through their officer, and the application must be filed at least 45 days before the departure date. A client who is granted international travel must surrender their passport to the Department on the day they return, and must check in within 1 working day of coming back.

## Emergency Travel

Travel that cannot be planned in advance, such as to attend the funeral of an immediate family member or to visit a gravely ill relative, may be approved by the Duty Supervisor outside the normal timelines. The client calls the Duty Line and the decision is made within 24 hours. The client must file Form T-7 within 3 working days after returning and provide proof of the emergency, such as an obituary or a letter from a hospital. Emergency permits are limited to 7 nights.

## Carrying the Permit

Clients must carry the approved permit, on paper or as a saved image in the HarborCheck app, for the whole trip and show it to any law enforcement officer who asks. Clients must check in by phone or app on the first day of travel and on the day of return. High-risk clients must also send a location message every 48 hours during the trip.

## Changes and Extensions

If a client wants to change the dates or the destination of an approved permit, they must call their officer before the original departure date. Changes of fewer than 3 days may be approved by phone and recorded by the officer. A client who cannot return by the permit end date because of illness, a vehicle breakdown or severe weather must call the officer before the permit expires and send supporting proof within 2 working days. An extension of up to 3 days may be granted once per permit.

## Denials and Appeals

An officer who denies a permit must give the reason in writing within 3 working days. The client may appeal to the unit supervisor within 5 working days of the denial. The supervisor decides within 5 working days and the decision is final unless the court order says otherwise. A client whose permit is denied twice for the same trip may ask the court to review the decision.
