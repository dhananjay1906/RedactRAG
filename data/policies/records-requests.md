# Records Requests Policy

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy ADM-310

## Purpose

This policy explains how members of the public, clients and other agencies may request copies of Department records, how long the Department takes to respond, what it costs, and which records are not available. The Department responds to all requests consistently and in writing.

## How to Make a Request

All requests must be made on Form R-3, which is available at the front desk, on the Department website, or by mail. A request may also be sent by email to the Records Unit. A request must describe the records sought clearly enough that staff can find them. A request that is too broad, for example "all records about my case," will be returned with a request for clarification, and the response clock stops until the requester replies.

Requesters must show identity when asking for records about themselves. The Department accepts two forms of identification, at least one of which must have a photo. Requests made on behalf of another person must include a signed authorization form dated within the last 90 days. Attorneys may submit a signed notice of appearance in place of an authorization.

## Response Times

The Records Unit acknowledges every request within 3 working days and assigns a tracking number. The Department must provide the records or a written denial within 10 working days of receiving a complete request. If the search requires reviewing a large volume of records or consulting with other agencies, the Department may extend the deadline once, by up to 15 additional working days, and must tell the requester about the extension and the reason before the first 10 days have passed.

Subpoenas and court orders are handled separately. The Records Unit responds to a valid subpoena within 5 working days of receipt, or by the return date on the subpoena if that is earlier.

Rush processing is available for a fee of $40. Rush requests are completed within 3 working days, but the Department may decline rush processing if the request involves more than 200 pages.

## Fees

Inspecting records in person at the Records Unit is free. Copies are charged as follows:

- The first 20 pages are free.
- Each additional page costs $0.25.
- A certified copy costs $5 per document, in addition to the page charge.
- Records provided electronically on a USB drive cost $10, and the Department supplies the drive.
- Records sent by secure download link are free.

A person may request one copy of their own supervision file once every 12 months at no charge. A second request in the same 12-month period is charged at the standard rate. Fees are waived for requests made by public defenders on behalf of indigent clients and by victims seeking records about the case in which they were harmed, to the extent the law allows. Payment must be received before copies are released, except for requests under $5, which are billed with the copies.

## Records That Are Not Available

The following records are exempt from release, in whole or in part:

- Juvenile records, unless the requester is the person named in the record and is over 18.
- The identity and contact information of victims and witnesses.
- Records of active investigations, until the investigation closes.
- Medical and mental health treatment records that came from a third-party provider, unless the client provides a signed release.
- Internal drafts, notes and attorney-client communications.
- Information that would reveal security procedures or the personal information of employees.

When part of a record is exempt, the Department releases the rest and marks each redaction with the reason. The requester receives a log listing the number of pages withheld and the exemption claimed for each.

## Appeals

A requester who is denied, or who believes the Department has withheld records improperly, may appeal in writing within 30 calendar days of the denial. The appeal goes to the Records Appeals Board, which meets on the second Thursday of each month. The Board issues its decision within 20 working days of the hearing. A requester who is still unsatisfied may file an action in the county court.

## Retention

Closed supervision files are kept for 7 years after the case closes, then destroyed by shredding. Drug testing results and laboratory reports are kept for 5 years. Visitor logs are kept for 1 year. Case files that are the subject of a pending records request or subpoena are not destroyed until the request is resolved. Files for persons whose records are expunged by court order must be sealed within 30 days of receiving the order.

## Request Tracking and Reporting

The Records Unit records every request in a log showing the date received, the tracking number, the fees collected and the date of the response. The log is reviewed each quarter by the Compliance Officer. A request that remains open after 40 working days is automatically escalated to the Chief Deputy.
