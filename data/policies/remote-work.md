# Remote Work Policy

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy HR-140

## Purpose

This policy describes when employees may work from home, what the Department provides, and what is expected of remote workers. Remote work is a privilege that the Department grants when it serves the needs of the Department and the people it supervises. It is not an employee right.

## Eligibility

An employee is eligible to apply for remote work after 12 months of continuous employment with the Department, and after finishing the probationary period. Employees must have a recent performance rating of "meets expectations" or higher and no active written reprimand. Employees on a performance improvement plan are not eligible.

Some jobs cannot be done remotely. Officers who conduct field visits, drug testing staff, front desk staff and anyone whose job needs daily access to paper files are not eligible for regular remote work, though they may qualify for occasional remote days to complete reports.

## Schedule

Eligible employees may work remotely up to 2 days per week. The days must be agreed in advance with the supervisor and may not include Tuesdays or Thursdays, which are in-person check-in days for client contact. Supervisors may approve a third remote day for one pay period in unusual cases, such as a long-term injury or a major construction project at the office.

Remote workers must be available during core hours, which are 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. Employees must answer phone calls and chat messages within 30 minutes during core hours, and email within 8 working hours. Outside of core hours, employees keep to their normal schedule unless their supervisor agrees to a different one.

## The Remote Work Agreement

Each remote employee signs Form RW-5 before starting. The agreement lists the approved days, the work address, the equipment issued and the security requirements. Form RW-5 expires after 6 months and must be renewed. The Department may end the agreement at any time with 5 working days of notice, and sooner if there is a performance or security concern. The employee may end it with 5 working days of notice as well.

The work address must be within 60 miles of the main office, so the employee can return to the office within 2 hours if needed. An employee who plans to work remotely from another address, including a relative's home, must update Form RW-5 first. Working remotely from outside the state, or from outside the country, is not allowed in any circumstance.

## Equipment and Stipends

The Department issues a laptop, a headset and a docking station to each remote employee. The Department pays a stipend of $45 per month toward home internet costs, paid with the regular paycheck. Employees receive a one-time ergonomic allowance of $300 for a chair, desk or monitor stand, which can be claimed once, with receipts, within 60 days of the first approved remote day. The Department does not pay for heating, electricity, furniture beyond the allowance or personal phones.

## Workspace and Security Requirements

The workspace must be a private room or area where screens and conversations cannot be seen or heard by other people. Employees must use the Department VPN for all work. Employees may not print case documents at home, take photos of screens or save files on personal devices. A locked cabinet is required for any Department equipment when it is not in use. Employees may not meet clients at their home, and may not accept deliveries of case documents there.

## Performance and Supervision

Remote workers are held to the same standards as office workers. Supervisors hold a check-in call with each remote worker once every week and review the remote worker's output after the first 90 days. If the review finds problems, the supervisor may reduce the number of remote days or end the agreement. A remote worker who does not respond within the response times more than twice in one month receives a written warning.

## Time Tracking

Remote employees record their start and end times in the timekeeping system each day. Non-exempt employees must take a 30-minute unpaid meal break after no more than 5 hours of work and may not work more than 8 hours in a day without prior written approval for overtime. Overtime worked remotely without approval may lead to discipline, though it is still paid.

## Emergency Remote Activation

When the Chief Probation Officer declares a weather emergency, a facility closure or a public health emergency, all employees whose jobs can be done remotely may be directed to work from home for the duration. In an emergency, the 2-day limit, the 12-month eligibility requirement and the agreement requirement are suspended, but all security rules remain in force. Employees are told to work remotely through the notification system by 6:30 a.m. on the day of the closure.
