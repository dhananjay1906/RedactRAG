# Victim Notification Policy

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy SUP-410

## Purpose

This policy explains how the Department tells registered victims about important changes in a client's supervision, how victims register for notices, and how victim information is protected. It applies to every case in which a victim has registered with the Department or the court has ordered notification.

## Registration

A victim may register for notification at any time while the client is under supervision. Registration is done on Form VN-2, which is available from the Victim Services Unit, the district attorney's office or the Department website. A victim may name up to 2 additional contacts, such as a family member or an advocate, to receive notices on their behalf.

Registration stays active until the client's supervision ends, unless the victim withdraws in writing. Victims must update their contact information within 10 working days of any change. The Department is not responsible for notices that cannot be delivered because contact information is out of date, but staff must make at least 3 attempts by different methods before recording a notice as undeliverable.

## Events That Trigger a Notice

The Department sends a notice to every registered victim when any of the following happens:

- The client's supervision level is reduced.
- The client is approved to transfer supervision to another county or state.
- The client receives a travel permit to the area where the victim lives or works.
- A warrant is issued for the client, or the client is arrested on a warrant.
- The client is scheduled for a violation hearing.
- The client's supervision ends early or is extended.

Notices about a warrant or an arrest are sent within 24 hours. All other notices are sent at least 5 working days before the change takes effect. If the change is ordered by the court with less notice, the Department sends the notice within 1 working day of receiving the order.

## How Notices Are Sent

Victims choose how they receive notices: by phone call, text message, email or letter. Phone calls are made between 8:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. unless the victim has asked for calls at any hour. Each notice states the type of event, the date it takes effect and the phone number of the Victim Services Unit. Notices never include the client's home address, workplace or treatment provider.

## Protecting Victim Information

Victim names, addresses and phone numbers are kept in a separate restricted section of the case file. Only Victim Services staff, the assigned officer and supervisors may view it. Victim information must never be shared with the client, the client's family or the client's attorney, and it is exempt from records requests under Policy ADM-310.

Any employee who accidentally discloses victim information must report the disclosure to their supervisor within 2 hours. The Victim Services Unit then contacts the victim within 24 hours to explain what happened and to offer a safety planning meeting.

## Protective Orders

When a protective order is in place, the officer checks at every contact that the client knows the terms of the order. A reported violation of a protective order is treated as a high-priority violation, and the officer must notify the court and the victim within 24 hours. If the victim asks, the Department may provide a letter confirming that the client is under supervision, for the victim to give to an employer or landlord.

## Victim Input

Registered victims may submit a written statement before any hearing on early termination or a reduction in supervision level. Statements must be received at least 3 working days before the hearing. The officer attaches every statement to the report sent to the court, without editing it.
