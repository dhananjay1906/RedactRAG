# Drug and Alcohol Testing Policy

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy SUP-240

## Purpose

This policy governs how the Department tests people under supervision for drugs and alcohol, how samples are collected and confirmed, and how results are used. Testing is ordered by the court or required by the supervision plan, and every test is documented in the case management system.

## Testing Methods

The Department uses urine testing as the standard method. Oral fluid testing is used when a client cannot provide a urine sample. Breath alcohol testing is used on site when an officer suspects recent drinking. Hair testing is used only on order of the court, or when a client has repeatedly submitted dilute samples.

The standard panel is a 10-panel screen covering amphetamines, methamphetamine, cocaine, opiates, oxycodone, marijuana metabolites, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, phencyclidine and methadone. An extended panel that adds synthetic cannabinoids and fentanyl is used for clients whose offense involved those substances.

## The Color Line

Clients on random testing call the Color Line every day after 6:00 a.m., including weekends and holidays. A recorded message lists the colors that are called that day. Each client is assigned one color at intake. If the client's color is called, they must report to a collection site within 4 hours of the time they heard the message, or before the site closes if that is sooner.

Failure to call the Color Line is recorded as a missed call. Three missed calls in a 30-day period count as one missed test. Clients who do not have a phone may use the kiosk in the lobby of Annex B.

## Collection Sites and Hours

The main collection site is open Monday to Friday from 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. and on Saturdays from 8:00 a.m. to 12:00 noon. The site is closed on Sundays and on county holidays, and on those days only clients ordered to provide a sample by a supervisor must report to the on-call site, which is open from 8:00 a.m. to 10:00 a.m.

A client must bring a photo ID. Clients without ID are asked to wait while staff confirm their identity from the case file. Clients must leave coats, bags and phones outside the collection room.

## Collection Procedure

Collection is observed by a staff member of the same sex as the client, or of the gender the client identifies with when this has been documented. The client has 3 hours after arriving to provide a sample, and may drink up to 8 ounces of water every 30 minutes during that period. If the client cannot produce a sample within 3 hours, the attempt is recorded as a refusal.

The collector checks the temperature of the sample within 4 minutes. The valid range is 90 to 100 degrees Fahrenheit. A sample outside that range, or a sample that is clearly altered, is recorded as an invalid sample, and a second sample must be collected under observation.

A dilute sample is one with creatinine below 20 mg/dL. One dilute result is recorded as inconclusive and the client is retested within 24 hours. Two dilute results in a 14-day period are treated as a positive result.

## Positive Results and Confirmation

A positive screen on site is considered preliminary. The sample is sent to the contract laboratory for confirmation by gas chromatography and mass spectrometry. The laboratory reports its result within 5 working days. A client who disputes a preliminary result may ask for confirmation at the client's own expense. The confirmation fee is $35, refunded if the result is overturned.

The following are treated as a positive result: a confirmed positive, a refusal to test, a substituted or adulterated sample, and leaving the site before a sample is given.

## Medications and Prescriptions

Clients must tell their officer about any prescription medication that can affect test results within 48 hours of beginning the medication. They should carry the pharmacy label or a letter from the prescriber. Clients should not stop taking a prescribed medication to avoid a positive result. A positive result that is fully explained by a documented prescription is recorded as negative. Poppy seeds and over-the-counter cold medicines do not excuse a positive result for opiates or methamphetamine.

## Fees

A fee of $12 is charged to the client for each test. The fee is waived for clients found indigent by the court and for clients enrolled in a Department-approved treatment program. Fees for an extended panel are $28. Hair testing costs $85 and is paid by the Department when ordered by the court.

## Response to a Positive Result

A positive result leads to a sanction conference with the officer and a supervisor within 7 working days. The conference decides among increased testing, a substance use assessment within 14 days, referral to treatment, or a referral to the court. A first positive result for marijuana at the low-risk level does not normally lead to a court referral. Three positive results in any 90-day period lead to a review by the Treatment Court team.
