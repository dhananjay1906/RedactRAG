# Check-In and Reporting Rules

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy SUP-210

## Purpose

This policy sets out how people under supervision must report to the Department, how officers document those contacts, and what happens when a check-in is missed. It applies to every person placed on supervision by the Harbor County courts.

## Supervision Levels and Check-In Frequency

Each person is assigned a risk level during the intake assessment, which must be completed within 10 working days of the first appointment. The risk level sets the minimum check-in frequency:

- Low risk: one check-in every month.
- Moderate risk: one check-in every 2 weeks.
- High risk: one check-in every week.

An officer may raise a person's level at any time after a documented incident. Lowering a level requires a supervisor's signature and at least 120 days of full compliance at the current level.

## Where and When to Check In

In-person check-ins take place at the main office or at Annex B on Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays between 9:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m. A person who arrives up to 15 minutes after their scheduled time is still marked as on time. Arrivals between 16 and 60 minutes late are marked as late and must be rescheduled within 2 working days. Arrivals more than 60 minutes late are treated as a missed check-in.

Low-risk clients who have completed 6 months without a violation may use the HarborCheck mobile app for up to 2 of every 3 check-ins. App check-ins require a photo and a location stamp. Moderate and high-risk clients must report in person at least every other time.

The kiosk in the lobby of Annex B is open from 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., Monday to Friday, and accepts address updates and fee payments, but it does not count as a check-in.

## Missed Check-Ins

A client who knows they will miss a check-in must contact their officer at least 24 hours in advance and ask for a new appointment. Excused absences include a documented medical emergency, a court appearance, a death in the immediate family, and a work shift that cannot be moved. Documentation must be provided within 3 working days.

When a client misses a check-in without an excuse, the officer must:

1. Attempt to reach the client by phone and text on the same day.
2. Send a written notice to the address on file within 1 working day.
3. Record the miss in the case management system within 24 hours.

After the second unexcused missed check-in in any 60-day period, the officer must file a violation report within 72 hours. After the third, the officer must request a supervisor review within 5 working days and may ask the court for a warrant. A person who contacts the Department voluntarily within 48 hours of a miss and explains the absence will have the first miss recorded as a warning only.

## Changes in Address, Employment or Contact Information

Clients must report any change of home address within 5 working days of moving. A change of employer, or the loss of a job, must be reported within 3 working days. A change of phone number must be reported within 2 working days. Failure to report these changes is a technical violation and is handled through the graduated response guide, not through an automatic court referral.

## Home Visits

Officers may make home visits to verify an address and living situation. Visits are normally announced at least 24 hours ahead for low and moderate-risk clients. High-risk clients may receive unannounced visits between 6:00 p.m. and 9:00 p.m., limited to 2 unannounced visits per month. An officer may not enter a residence without the consent of an adult who lives there, unless the court order specifically permits it. Every home visit must be recorded in the case notes within 24 hours.

## Progress Reports to the Court

Officers file a written progress report with the sentencing court every 90 days. The report covers attendance, test results, employment, program participation and fee status. A copy is given to the client on request. A supervisor must review and sign each report before it is filed, and no report may be filed more than 7 days after its due date.

## Graduated Responses

Not every violation leads to a court referral. The graduated response guide lists the following steps in order:

- Verbal warning, documented in the case notes.
- Written warning and a required meeting with a supervisor.
- Increased check-in frequency for 30 days.
- Community service of 8 to 24 hours.
- Referral to the court.

An officer may skip a step only with written approval from the supervisor, and only for violations involving safety concerns or new criminal charges.

## Early Termination

A client may be considered for early termination of supervision after completing half of the supervised term, paying all required fees, and maintaining 12 consecutive months without a violation. The officer submits the recommendation to the supervisor, who forwards it to the court within 15 working days.
