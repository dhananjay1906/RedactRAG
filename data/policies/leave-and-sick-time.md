# Leave and Sick Time Policy

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy HR-110

## Purpose and Scope

This policy explains how employees of the Harbor County Probation Department earn, request and use paid and unpaid leave. It applies to all full-time and part-time employees, including probation officers, clerical staff and supervisors. Temporary employees hired for fewer than 6 months are covered only by the sick time section and the jury duty section.

Part-time employees who work at least 20 hours per week earn leave at half the rates listed below. Employees who work fewer than 20 hours per week earn sick time only, at the rate of 1 hour for every 30 hours worked.

## Sick Time Accrual

Full-time employees earn 8 hours of sick time per month, which equals 96 hours (12 working days) per year. Sick time is credited on the last day of each pay period that falls in the month.

Unused sick time carries over from one calendar year to the next, but the carryover balance is capped at 40 hours. Any balance above 40 hours on December 31 is converted to a one-time wellness credit of $15 per forfeited hour, paid in the first January paycheck. The wellness credit is limited to 56 forfeited hours.

Sick time may be used for the employee's own illness or injury, medical appointments, and the care of a spouse, child or parent. It may not be used for vacation, and it may not be borrowed against future accrual.

## Reporting an Absence

An employee who cannot report to work must notify their direct supervisor by phone or text no later than 7:30 a.m. on the first day of absence. Email alone is not accepted for the first day. An employee who does not notify a supervisor and does not appear for 2 consecutive shifts is considered to have abandoned the position, and a notice is sent to the employee's address on file.

A medical note from a licensed provider is required when an absence lasts more than 3 consecutive working days. The note must state the expected return date. A fitness-for-duty clearance is required after any absence of 10 or more consecutive working days.

## Annual Leave (Vacation)

Annual leave accrues according to length of service:

- Less than 5 years of service: 10 working days per year.
- 5 to 9 years of service: 15 working days per year.
- 10 or more years of service: 20 working days per year.

Annual leave balances may not exceed 1.5 times the annual rate. Once an employee reaches that ceiling, no further leave accrues until the balance drops. Employees may carry over a maximum of 30 days into the next calendar year.

Requests for annual leave of 3 days or more must be submitted on Form L-14 at least 14 calendar days before the first requested day. Supervisors must approve or deny the request in writing within 5 working days. When two employees request the same dates, the employee with the earlier submission date receives priority, except that no more than 2 officers per unit may be on annual leave at the same time.

## Family and Medical Leave

An employee who has worked for the Department for at least 12 months and for at least 1,250 hours in the previous 12 months may take up to 12 weeks of unpaid family and medical leave in a rolling 12-month period. Leave may be taken for the birth or adoption of a child, a serious health condition of the employee, or the serious health condition of a spouse, child or parent.

The employee must give 30 days of notice when the need for leave is foreseeable. The Department continues to pay its share of health insurance premiums during the leave. Employees must use any accrued sick time before the unpaid portion of the leave begins, but are not required to use annual leave.

## Bereavement Leave

Employees receive 5 paid working days for the death of a spouse, child, parent or sibling. They receive 3 paid working days for the death of a grandparent, in-law or other extended family member. Bereavement days must be taken within 30 days of the date of death or of the memorial service. Employees who must travel more than 300 miles may request 2 additional unpaid days.

## Donated Leave Pool

Employees may donate unused annual leave, in blocks of 4 hours, to a shared pool. The pool is open to employees who have exhausted all of their own leave because of a serious medical condition. A single recipient may receive no more than 240 hours from the pool in any 12-month period. Donations are anonymous, and a donor may not receive anything in return.

## Jury Duty and Court Appearances

An employee called for jury duty receives full pay for up to 10 working days per calendar year. After that, the employee may use annual leave or take unpaid leave. Employees subpoenaed as witnesses in matters unrelated to their work receive the same 10-day allowance. Employees who testify as part of their official duties are on duty and do not use leave.

## Unpaid Leave

A supervisor may approve unpaid personal leave of up to 30 calendar days for employees with at least 2 years of service. Longer unpaid leave requires approval from the Chief Probation Officer. Unpaid leave does not count toward the length of service used to determine annual leave tiers once it exceeds 30 days.

## Payout at Separation

Employees who leave the Department are paid for unused annual leave up to a maximum of 30 days. Unused sick time is not paid out at separation, except that employees who retire with at least 15 years of service receive a payout of 25 percent of their sick time balance, up to a limit of 20 days.
