# Code of Conduct

Harbor County Probation Department, Policy HR-120

## Purpose and Scope

This code sets the standards of behavior expected of every Department employee, volunteer and contractor. Probation work gives staff authority over people's lives and access to sensitive information, so the Department holds its staff to standards that go beyond those of ordinary workplaces. Violations may lead to discipline up to and including termination.

## Professional Relationships with Clients

Employees must keep relationships with clients professional. Employees may not enter into a romantic, financial or business relationship with a person under supervision, or with a client's immediate family member. An employee who has a pre-existing personal relationship with a client, such as a relative or former neighbor, must disclose it to a supervisor within 2 working days of learning that the person is a client, and the case will be reassigned.

Employees may not give clients money, rides or personal favors. Employees may not accept anything of value from a client. Meetings with clients take place at Department offices or approved community locations, never at an employee's home.

## Gifts and Hospitality

An employee may not accept gifts from any person or organization that does business with the Department, or that is under its supervision. The only exceptions are small items of nominal value such as a coffee mug or a calendar, and meals at an event that the employee attends in an official capacity. The total value of anything accepted from one source may not exceed $25 in a calendar year. Any gift that exceeds the limit must be returned or turned over to the Ethics Officer within 5 working days, and all gifts must be recorded in the gift register.

## Conflicts of Interest

An employee who has a financial interest, family relationship or outside role that could affect their judgment on a Department matter must disclose it in writing within 10 working days of becoming aware of it. The employee must step away from any decision affected by the conflict until the Ethics Officer has reviewed it. An annual disclosure statement is due on February 15 each year.

## Outside Employment

Employees need approval for any paid work outside the Department. Approval is requested on Form E-2 and must be renewed every year. Approval will not be given where the outside job creates a conflict with Department duties, takes place during working hours, involves work for the courts, law enforcement or a treatment provider that serves clients, or exceeds 20 hours per week. Employees may not use Department equipment or information for outside work.

## Reporting Misconduct

Every employee has a duty to report suspected misconduct, including excessive force, falsified records, sexual harassment, discrimination, theft and misuse of authority. Reports must be made within 24 hours of the employee learning of the conduct. Reports can be made to a supervisor, to the Ethics Officer, or anonymously to the Integrity Hotline. Retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith is itself a serious violation and may result in immediate termination.

The Ethics Officer acknowledges a report within 2 working days, decides whether to open an investigation within 10 working days, and aims to complete investigations within 60 calendar days. The person who made the report receives a summary of the outcome, to the extent confidentiality rules allow.

## Use of Authority and Force

Officers may use only the force that is reasonable and necessary to protect themselves or others. Every use of force must be reported in writing before the end of the shift. Officers must complete 8 hours of de-escalation training every year, and 4 hours of ethics training, both by December 31. Officers who have not completed their training by that date are not allowed to supervise clients until they do.

## Confidentiality and Social Media

Employees may not share information about clients or cases with anyone who is not authorized to receive it. This duty continues after employment ends. Employees may not post photos, names or stories about clients on social media, even if the details seem anonymous. Employees who identify themselves as Department staff online must make clear that their views are personal.

## Fitness for Duty

Employees must report for work free from the effects of alcohol or illegal drugs. A supervisor who has reasonable suspicion that an employee is impaired may order a fitness-for-duty evaluation. An employee who is arrested or charged with a criminal offense must notify the Chief Probation Officer within 1 working day.

## Progressive Discipline

The Department normally uses the following steps, in order, for violations that are not serious enough to justify immediate termination:

1. Verbal counseling, documented in the supervisor's file.
2. Written reprimand.
3. Suspension without pay for up to 3 working days.
4. Demotion or a longer suspension of up to 10 working days.
5. Termination.

Conduct that involves violence, theft, falsification of records, sexual misconduct or being under the influence while on duty may be treated as grounds for immediate termination. An employee has 10 working days to appeal any written reprimand or higher penalty to the Personnel Board.

## Post-Employment Restrictions

For 12 months after leaving the Department, former employees may not lobby or represent others before the Department on any matter they worked on during their last 2 years of employment.
